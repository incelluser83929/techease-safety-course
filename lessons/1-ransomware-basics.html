Ransomware Basics

What it is, how it gets onto a device, and why it targets everyday people.

What is ransomware?

Ransomware is malicious software ("malware") that locks you out of your own files or device — usually by encrypting them so they can't be opened — and then demands payment to restore access. The demand often comes as an on-screen note asking for payment in cryptocurrency, with a countdown clock to add pressure.

How does it get onto a device?

Ransomware almost always needs you to do something first. The most common ways it spreads:

  • Opening an attachment or clicking a link in an unexpected email
  • Downloading "free" software, movies, or files from untrustworthy websites
  • Clicking a fake pop-up claiming your device needs an urgent "update" or "cleanup"
  • Plugging in an infected USB drive
  • Criminals exploiting an out-of-date operating system or app that hasn't been patched

⚠️ Should you pay the ransom?

  • Paying does not guarantee your files will be returned
  • It funds criminal activity and can mark you as a willing target for future attacks
  • Law enforcement (including the FBI) generally advises against paying

Who does it target?

Ransomware doesn't just target big companies and hospitals — it targets anyone with a device and files worth protecting: photos, financial records, tax documents, or a small business's customer list. Attackers cast a wide net, so anyone can be a target.

✅ Quick tips

  • Back up your important files regularly (covered in detail in Module 4)
  • Keep your operating system and apps updated
  • Never click links or open attachments from unexpected or unfamiliar messages
  • Use antivirus/security software and keep it turned on
  • Know who to call for help before you need it — a trusted family member, TechEase, or a professional
