What To Do If You're a Victim

The exact steps to take — calmly and without shame.

💙 First: it is not your fault

Scams and ransomware are designed by professional criminals to fool smart, careful people. Falling for one doesn't mean you did something wrong — the most important thing now is acting quickly and reporting it, not feeling ashamed.

If you suspect ransomware on a device

  1. Disconnect it from the internet — turn off Wi-Fi or unplug the network cable. This can stop the ransomware from spreading further or communicating with attackers.
  2. Don't pay the ransom and don't panic-click anything on the ransom screen.
  3. Don't try to fix it alone if you're not comfortable — get help from a trusted tech-savvy family member, TechEase, or a professional.
  4. Once the device is cleaned (professionally, if needed), restore your files from your backup.
  5. Change your passwords from a different, unaffected device — starting with email and banking.

If you sent money or shared information to a scammer

  1. Contact your bank or card company immediately to report the transaction and ask about reversing it or freezing the account.
  2. Change passwords for any account where you may have shared login details.
  3. Turn on two-factor authentication if you haven't already (see Module 3).
  4. Report it — see below.

Where to report

  • FBI Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3): ic3.gov
  • Federal Trade Commission (FTC): reportfraud.ftc.gov
  • Your local police non-emergency line, especially if money was lost
  • Your bank or credit card company, if financial information was involved
Reporting doesn't just help you — it helps investigators track patterns and warn others. Even if you don't expect your money back, a report matters.

✅ Quick tips

  • Act quickly, but don't panic — disconnect, don't pay, and get help
  • Report the incident, even if you feel embarrassed
  • Lean on your backup if ransomware struck — this is exactly what it's for
  • Talk to someone you trust; you don't have to handle it alone
