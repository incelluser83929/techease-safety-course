Recognizing Phishing & Scam Calls

Spot the red flags in emails, texts, and phone calls before they cost you.

What is phishing?

Phishing is a fake message — email, text, or phone call — that pretends to come from a trusted source, like your bank, Amazon, Social Security, or even a family member, in order to trick you into clicking a link, sharing personal information, or sending money.

Common red flags

  • Urgency and fear: "Your account will be closed in 24 hours" or "Suspicious activity detected"
  • Requests for gift cards or wire transfers — no legitimate company or agency asks to be paid this way
  • An unfamiliar sender address that looks close to, but not quite, the real company's
  • Generic greetings like "Dear Customer" instead of your name
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes
  • Links that don't match the real company's website when you hover over them
  • Unsolicited pop-ups or calls claiming your computer has a virus
"This is Microsoft support calling. We've detected a virus on your computer. Please allow us to remotely connect so we can remove it." — This is a classic tech support scam. Microsoft, Apple, and similar companies do not make unsolicited calls like this.

Scams that specifically target seniors

  • Tech support scams: a fake pop-up or call claims your device is infected
  • Grandparent scams: a caller pretends to be a grandchild in an emergency, asking for money urgently and secretly
  • Social Security / Medicare scams: callers claim your benefits are suspended unless you "verify" personal information
  • Romance scams: an online relationship develops over time, then the person asks for money for a crisis

✅ Quick tips

  • Never click links in unexpected emails or texts — go to the company's website directly instead
  • Hang up and call the company or agency back using a number you look up yourself
  • Verify with a family member by phone before sending money for an "emergency"
  • Never give a stranger remote access to your computer
  • When in doubt, pause and ask someone you trust before responding
